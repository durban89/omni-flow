## Medical disclaimer

CondomIQ publishes general educational information about condoms and sexual health. It is **not medical advice**, it is not a diagnosis, and reading it does not create a doctor–patient relationship with anyone.

We cannot assess your personal situation — your anatomy, allergies, medication, medical history or the circumstances of a specific exposure. Two readers with the same question can need different answers. If you need a decision about your own health, talk to a qualified clinician, pharmacist or sexual health service.

**Seek professional care promptly if you have:**

- a suspected condom failure (breakage, slippage, or a condom that came off) during sex with a partner whose STI status you do not know;
- pain, swelling, rash, itching or numbness that appeared after using a condom or lubricant — this can indicate a latex allergy or irritation;
- symptoms of an STI, such as unusual discharge, sores, burning when urinating, or pelvic pain;
- any concern about pregnancy risk.

Emergency contraception and post-exposure care are time-sensitive: the sooner you are seen, the more options you have. If you are in immediate danger or severe pain, contact your local emergency number.

**No method is 100% effective.** Condoms are medical devices regulated by bodies such as the FDA (US), the MHRA (UK), Health Canada, and the TGA (Australia). When used correctly and consistently they substantially reduce — but do not eliminate — the risk of pregnancy and sexually transmitted infections. Figures quoted on this site describe population-level typical use and will not predict your individual outcome.

**Fit and material advice is general.** Suitability depends on the individual. If a clinician has told you to avoid a material, or you have a diagnosed allergy, follow their advice rather than anything published here.

**Content is written with AI assistance.** Drafts are produced with AI tools and edited for accuracy against the sources cited on each page. We can still make mistakes. Every article lists its sources so you can check our reasoning, and we correct errors when they are reported.

Some links on this site are **affiliate links**. If you click one and buy something, the retailer may pay us a small commission. **You pay the same price** — the commission comes out of the retailer's margin, not your pocket.

This is how the site is funded. It does not buy influence:

- We do not accept payment for coverage, for a positive review, or for a particular placement.
- Commission rates do not affect which products we mention, how we describe them, or the order in which they appear.
- Where we recommend something, we say why, and where we note a drawback, it is a real one.
- Products we have not tested are described as such. We do not claim hands-on testing we have not done.

Affiliate links are marked on the page with a disclosure line directly beneath the link, in line with the US FTC's guidance on endorsements and testimonials (16 CFR Part 255) and the UK CAP Code rules on affiliate marketing. Links carry `rel="sponsored nofollow"`, so they are not treated as editorial endorsements by search engines.

Prices, availability and product specifications change constantly. Always confirm the details on the retailer's page before buying. We are not the seller, we do not process orders, and we cannot help with delivery, returns, refunds or warranty claims — those are handled by the retailer you bought from.
